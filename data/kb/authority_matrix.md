## Mandatory approval categories

Pricing, discounts, warranty, indemnity, security exceptions, roadmap dates, service credits, and new uptime SLA commitments require human approval. The same requirement applies when a proposed response changes an established product-availability boundary, data-residency term, retention period, deployment model, or supported integration.

## Decision rights

Proposal teams may accurately restate approved, current evidence within its documented scope. Product owners approve new product or roadmap commitments. Security owners approve control exceptions and unverified assurance statements. Delivery owners approve customer-specific implementation dates. Legal or commercial owners approve contractual remedies, warranties, indemnities, pricing, discounts, and service credits.

## Evidence is necessary but not sufficient

Sufficient evidence does not remove a mandatory approval requirement. A current source can establish what the standard position is, while the authority matrix separately determines whether a customer-specific promise may be made.

Unsupported categorical yes answers must be qualified, rejected, or escalated. Conflicting approved evidence must be presented for review rather than resolved by choosing the more convenient value.
